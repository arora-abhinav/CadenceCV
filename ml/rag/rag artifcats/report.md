**What this suggests**

Your foot strike angles (44–48°) are well outside the range typically seen even in exaggerated rearfoot-strike testing. In one lab study of 19 habitual rearfoot strikers, their normal strike angle averaged 14°, and even when researchers *instructed* an intentionally deeper heel strike, the group only reached about 29° (Han, 2025, imposed-condition study, n=19). Your values sit clearly above that. This lines up with the fact that your shin-based overstride check flagged nearly every stride on both legs — a small IMU study (Baker, 2024, n=10) found that landing with more than 10° of dorsiflexion was associated with greater overstriding distance. Interestingly, your center-of-mass overstride measure showed none of these strides as problematic, suggesting this is showing up specifically in the lower-leg/foot landing posture rather than in overall body position relative to the ground — a distinction that's easier to confirm with an in-person look at your shank and foot than from video metrics alone.

Your knee and hip angles otherwise look symmetric, and your trunk lean sits close to a value that one observational comparison of runners linked to efficient technique (Carson, 2024, citing Williams & Cavanagh) — though that was a between-runner comparison, not a test of causation, so it's a descriptive note rather than something to actively chase.

**What to do**

Since there's no pain or stated goal driving this analysis, we're not recommending a gait change from these numbers alone. The one thing worth flagging for a coach or physio to look at directly is the combination of very steep foot strike angle and near-constant shin-based overstride flags on both sides — that pattern is unusual enough to be worth a hands-on look, particularly if you ever notice shin, knee, or foot soreness developing, since studies manipulating foot strike show that more extreme, non-habitual strike patterns increase loading on the lower leg (Han, 2025). If you're curious whether this is a one-off or a consistent trait, repeating this same capture on another run and comparing the foot strike numbers would be a simple way to check.
